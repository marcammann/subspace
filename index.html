P.S.

Swiss-born. Built things in Zurich, Brooklyn, Portland, and now Bozeman.

I'm currently VP of AI Product at Thrive Global, building health coaching systems for enterprise clients like Microsoft and Eli Lilly. Before that, I was the founding architect at ThriveAI Health, Thrive's OpenAI-backed venture, where I helped take the platform from napkin sketch to something real: conversational, contextual, clinically grounded, and careful enough to be trusted.

Before Thrive, I co-founded Matter Supply Co. in Portland with a charming amount of hubris and a very sincere belief that good work comes from good conditions. We were values- and principles-driven from the beginning, inspired by cultures like Pixar and Netflix, and then educated by the slower, harder business of actually building one ourselves.

Over eight years, Matter grew from zero to 50 people and $10M in ARR. We built digital products and experiential systems for Nike, Sweetgreen, United We Dream, and others. I led work across technology, design, and delivery: Nike DNA, a 250,000+ artifact archive for the Department of Nike Archives; the Nike DNA Museum Experience; Colin Kaepernick's "Dream Crazy" real-time experience; Backends for high traffic experiences like Air Max Vote Back and Vote Forward; Sweetgreen's unified GraphQL API & Android app; and Notifica, a mobile tool for immigrant communities preparing for and responding to ICE encounters.

But the real work was not (just) shipping projects. It was building a team that could keep shipping without me holding the ceiling up: growing leads into owners, and helping engineers and designers become product builders, decision makers, and leaders.

Before Matter, I was Technology Director for Emerging Technologies at Huge's innovation lab in Portland, running Nike experiential technology and managing teams across New York, Portland, and Medellín. Before that, I worked at Liip in Zurich while studying computer science at ETH, where I built Switzerland's first public transit iPhone app and accidentally helped start a national conversation about open data. Before that, I was a 14-year-old developer writing PHP and CFML pages, which is one way to learn humility early.

Everywhere I've been, the pattern has been the same: fast, effective teams that ship. Not frantic teams. Not heroic teams. Teams with taste, trust, standards, and enough candor to let the first idea die with dignity.

The lesson I keep coming back to is that culture is not what you announce. It is what you tolerate. Standards live in the small decisions, especially the inconvenient ones.

When the map and the territory disagree, trust the territory. Be rigorous, not ruthless. Lead when you have to, and let others lead when you can. People are more than what they create, but mediocre work is still bad for the soul.

No heroes. No masters. Work the problem.
